State Tipping-Point Analysis (Razor-Edge Scenario · NDA +1pp)

Which state decides the election?

Greedy pivotality: % of hung simulations where this state's NDA seats are in the minimum set needed to flip the outcome to an NDA majority. Based on 3,540 hung simulations at 543 seats.
Why UP dominates at 99.9%: Uttar Pradesh has 80 seats — the largest state by far — and a near-50/50 split (36 NDA, 43 INDIA in 2024). With a +1pp NDA lead nationally, UP's NDA seat count alone swings between ~35 and ~50, easily covering the typical 5–20 seat deficit from quota 272. Maharashtra is secondary (0.3%) only when UP's swing partially but incompletely fills the gap.

⚠ Model caveats — read before citing

D1 · Seat-share proxy: Dirichlet means use 2024 seat shares (~52.9% NDA), not real vote shares (~36–46%). P(majority) numbers are upward-biased in absolute level; directional findings are robust.
D2 · Variance too tight: Concentration=18 gives ±11.5pp per-seat std. Real TCPD data shows ±21pp (correct ≈ 5). Winner's bonus understated ~1.8×.
D3 · Not a forecast: Anchored to 2024. Political realignments, new alliances, delimitation outcomes not modelled.
Tipping-point metric: Greedy pivotality overweights large states (UP 80 seats). It answers "which state's seats cover the deficit?" not "which state is most swingable?" — a useful but incomplete measure.
